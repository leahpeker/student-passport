# Cognitive Task Analysis: Student deshawn (Grade 9) — School Year 2025–2026

## Snapshot

Across 17 sessions over eight months, Deshawn Carter's AI tutor use has one clear shape: almost every session exists because he was about to miss something or already had. Early-morning messages sent around 6:40am, before the school day starts, ask what he'll miss or how to catch up fast — and the catching-up itself is real work, not just a summary handed to him: he reconstructs why meiosis has to halve the chromosome count, predicts an angle relationship before being told it, and once diagnoses on his own that three different subjects need three different kinds of review. His clearest strength is a kind of efficient self-repair — rebuilding a gap in his understanding quickly and correctly once he has a few minutes with the tutor. The single most important thing to know: the one time this broke down was under real time pressure the night before a History test, when he asked for a full set of study-guide answers rather than working through them himself — and that class's next exam was his lowest score of the year. The rest of the picture, including a fully self-designed studio art piece and an unprompted structural connection between a geometry proof and a history source-evaluation method, shows a student thinking clearly whenever the clock allows it.

**Evidence base:** 91 student turns across 17 sessions spanning September 2025 to May 2026, across six subject areas. Enough breadth and duration to support a genuine profile, including which patterns are habitual versus one-off.

## What the evidence shows

### Metacognitive — the most frequent type, almost all of it forward-planning

This is the type that most directly reflects how Deshawn manages his own schedule. Before a Geometry class he was going to miss, he opened by asking what he'd be missing rather than catching up afterward: "what did geometry cover today, im not going to be there" (sess_7a2c9e05, 2025-11-03). Two weeks before finals, he named the actual shape of his problem across three classes at once: "bio and geometry i think i understand it when i catch up, its history where i actually dont get some of it, not just missed days" (T82, sess_7a2c9e08) — correctly separating a content gap from an understanding gap before being asked to. He then built and tested a cause-and-effect study method for history in the same session, unprompted. A smaller instance shows the same self-advocacy turned toward his own logistics: rather than accept losing lunch to a make-up test again, he worked out that a before-school slot might work instead, since he's already up that early (sess_7a2c9e13, 2026-02-23).

### Conceptual — his clearest strength once he's caught up

The strongest single instance in the corpus is a repaired mental model, not a delivered fact. Missing a Biology lesson on mitosis versus meiosis, he started with a half-right guess and built the actual reason from there: "growing/repairing vs making eggs and sperm. so meiosis has to cut the chromosome number in half so when they combine it goes back to normal" (T22, sess_7a2c9e02, 2025-09-29). The same move shows up in Geometry seven days later: given two angle relationships and asked to guess a third, he predicted it correctly by reasoning rather than asking — "if the other ones are equal maybe these add up to something instead. 180?" (T37, sess_7a2c9e03). A third instance, in April, shows him catching his own conceptual slip mid-answer during a Punnett-square self-test and correcting it without help (sess_7a2c9e15).

### Procedural — reliable, with the gap being attendance, not ability

His procedural work is consistently correct once content is in front of him: full triangle-congruence proofs brought in for a check (sess_7a2c9e06), a similar-triangles ratio problem solved and explained the same day he first learned the method (sess_7a2c9e05), a parallelogram proof checked from the library ten minutes before closing (sess_7a2c9e16). The peak instance is the Punnett-square session, where he stops himself mid-calculation — "wait no there is no WW in incomplete dominance thats codominance im mixing them up" — and re-derives the correct cross immediately after (T155–157, sess_7a2c9e15).

### Creative — strongest when he generates first

In a Studio Art session, asked to make a piece about a place that matters to him, he supplied the entire concept himself: the kitchen at 6am before anyone else is up, the feeling he wanted ("calm but also kind of alone"), and the one element of motion in an otherwise still scene — "steam. like from the kettle. that could be the one moving thing in a still picture" (T121, sess_7a2c9e11). The same pattern shows up in an English session that opened very differently: "can u just write the opening paragraph and ill go from there" (T71, sess_7a2c9e07). The tutor declined and asked what moment he'd actually thought about writing before. He landed on taking over his sister's morning routine, named what it changed in him, and then wrote his own opening line: "nobody told me the morning was mine now. it just was, one day, and i had to figure out what that meant." (T77). The thinking only happened because the tutor didn't comply with the opening request.

### Critical Thinking — rare, and fully realized the one time it appears

Preparing for a DBQ with two self-interested sources describing the same labor strike, he worked past "neither is neutral" into a usable method: "no i think you can still use both, you just gotta say why each one might be slanted. like use the newspaper for what actually happened logistically but not for who to blame" (T130, sess_7a2c9e12). Asked what the two documents did agree on, he found the more solid ground himself: they agreed on the date and that people were hurt, and disagreed only on blame — "oh thats smart, ill use that in the essay" (T134).

### Transfer — appears once, and it's a real one

In May, reviewing an old Geometry proof, he noticed unprompted that its logic matched something from a different class entirely: "like in the proof you use two different facts (alternate angles and corresponding angles) that both independently prove the lines are parallel. and with the history documents, two sources that disagree on blame still both independently confirmed the same date and injuries. its like using two paths to the same answer to make it more solid" (T166, sess_7a2c9e17). Asked where else that pattern might show up, he found a third instance on his own, from Biology: "maybe like when you check a bio answer two different ways to make sure you didnt mess up. thats the same thing i do without thinking about it" (T168) — recognizing a habit he already had, once it was named.

### Fluency — mostly deliberate self-quizzing

Fluency shows up as confident, self-initiated drilling — organelle functions, colonial vocabulary, DNA replication enzymes — where he runs himself through recall and self-corrects when needed (sess_7a2c9e01, sess_7a2c9e04, sess_7a2c9e10). It is appropriately shallow, since fluency is supposed to be fast and automatic; the one place it turns concerning is covered below.

## Where thinking was offloaded

There is exactly one clean offload in the whole corpus, and it stands out because everything around it doesn't look like it. The night before a History test, with three sections of the study guide left, he tried briefly: "test is tomorrow and i still have like 3 sections of the study guide left. can u just give me the answers so i can copy them down" (sess_7a2c9e14, 2026-03-16). The tutor offered to give him the first answer and let him find the pattern for the rest. He declined that offer under real time pressure — "i really dont have time tonight, my night got messed up. can u just give me all of them" — and the tutor gave him all three sections. He copied them down with no follow-up questions. His History unit exam three days later, on 2026-03-19, was his lowest score of the year (79), on a subject where his other engagement was otherwise strong.

## What's missing

Exploratory work is entirely absent from this corpus — not thin, but zero. Every one of his 17 sessions is tied to an assignment, a test, or an absence; there is no session where he simply follows a curiosity with no due date attached. Given the pattern of early-morning and after-school-library sessions visible throughout, this reads less like disinterest and more like a schedule with very little slack in it.

Critical Thinking and Transfer are both thin in frequency but strong in the one instance each provides — the gap here is one of occasion, not ability, and matches a pattern worth watching rather than worrying about: a proven capability that hasn't yet become a habit.

## For the teacher

**Watch the 2026-03-16 pattern specifically.** Under real time pressure the night before a History test, he asked for and got a full set of study-guide answers with no follow-up, and his score on that unit exam (79) was his lowest of the year. A standing check-in for that class — flag what's unfinished by Wednesday, not the night before — could catch the next one before it happens. This is the single most concrete, time-bound thing to change, and it's isolated to one class.

**Name and reinforce the study strategies he's already inventing.** He built a real cause-and-effect study method for history on his own (2025-12-15) and a scheduling fix for his make-up tests (2026-02-23, asking for a before-school slot instead of losing lunch). Ask him directly, once, whether either is still working — he won't bring it up unprompted, but he clearly does this kind of problem-solving already.

**Give him one no-stakes, curiosity-driven question when the schedule allows it.** Every session in this corpus is assignment- or catch-up-driven. His Studio Art and Transfer sessions show he thinks clearly and originally the moment he has room to; he just doesn't seem to get much room.

**Prompt Transfer on purpose, occasionally.** He connected a geometry proof to a history source-evaluation method and then to how he checks his own biology work, entirely unprompted, in a single May session. Ask him roughly monthly whether something from one class reminds him of another — the capability is proven and just needs more occasions to run.

## Caveats

This is one AI tutor on one platform across one school year — it doesn't capture in-class work, paper homework, tests, or conversations with teachers and peers. Sessions are self-selected: he brought these conversations to the tutor almost always early morning before school or in the library in the afternoon, which skews the sample toward catch-up and homework-shaped tasks rather than however he engages with material when he isn't behind. Depth ratings reflect what he wrote in the moment, not independently verified retention. The single offloading instance is real but isolated to one evening and one subject — one instance in 17 sessions is a flagged event, not yet a demonstrated pattern.
